# Rooftops and sky

The portfolio uses warm paper, olive ink, peach plaster, butter, mint, and powder blue. Traditional projecting balconies have carved wood, arched bays, carved diamond rails, cornices, and brackets, drawn with crisp silhouettes and flat shading. Plaster, brickwork, shutters, planted pots, laundry, and a pavilion retain the Lahore rooftop setting. Architectural signboards and all building lettering are removed. Existing English/Urdu name treatments and portfolio typography remain.

The Minar is shortened to approximately 60% of its former height, anchored to the rooftop baseline. The skyline uses a 2000×320 canvas, uniform aspect-ratio scaling, and 32 varied foreground/background building facades. Increasing the canvas width narrows the original skyline horizontally and leaves room for additional buildings without flattening their geometry. The Minar also scales uniformly. Night pairs slate blue with ivory and sage. Sun and moon share a circular orbit; its desktop size also follows viewport height to avoid the header. Reduced motion switches effectively immediately.

There are eight kites at widths of 768px and above, five below. A seeded best-candidate sampler produces a fresh, spaced sky on each visit. Individual destinations, speeds, banking, and wind responses replace synchronized CSS drift. An occasional loop, swoop, or line-cut/fall occurs every 8–14 seconds after the first twirl at 4–7 seconds. Fallen ambient kites fully leave the viewport, then replacements start invisible at a sky edge and fade in over 1.6 seconds. Pause and reduced motion freeze ambient simulation. Kites remain behind text at 16% opacity over text and 72% elsewhere.

Click, tap, drag, or Enter/Space claims a kite and starts immediately in the same sky, preserving identity and position. Opponents have their own tension, line strength, and attack rhythms, and can eliminate each other or the player. Sustained crossings create warning time before damage; the strongest incoming attack is applied rather than stacking all crossed lines. Release lowers tension and reduces incoming damage; evasion breaks contact. Eliminated kites fall away without respawning during play. The player loses when their line breaks and wins only after every opponent is eliminated. Simultaneous player/final-opponent elimination is a loss.

Portfolio content hides during play. One rounded bottom control box carries remaining opponents, crossing danger, tension, line strength, pull, pause/resume, exit, and inline results/replay. Arrows or dragging steer; held dragging, the pull button, or Space pulls; release or D adds slack. Escape exits. Backgrounding pauses. Pause freezes simulation timers. Replay preserves the selected kite and original round positions with fresh bot behavior. Leaving releases ownership and restores focus to the selected kite. Reduced-motion gameplay uses slower flight and no tricks or animated falling.

At desktop viewports of at least 1280×720, each chapter fits one screen without nested or internal scrolling. All five projects retain descriptions, details, and technologies; the HTTP detail is shortened to clear the Minar. Projects use three columns throughout desktop layouts. Wheel input anywhere in the scene advances between complete chapters, including when over a kite. Smaller windows keep natural, readable scrolling; mobile layout refinement remains deferred. Home returns to /.

The simulation lives separately from the React controls and uses seeded randomness for reproducible checks. No external API, rendering dependency, or raster asset was added.

Content surfaces are transparent and shadow-free so kites remain visible behind entire sections. The side geometric frames are removed; the header frieze remains. Sun and moon live in an independent, non-interactive layer above the header and below content, retaining the circular transition. Drift speeds are reduced to 20–42 normalized units/second with longer 5–10 second glides; tricks last roughly 4–5 seconds. Ambient events cycle through twirls, falls, and swoops to ensure visible variety without starting play. The viewport-fit layout also covers desktop widths from 1200px and heights from 700px.

Architecture and celestial bodies fade out when leaving Home and return on Home; kites remain softly behind content. The moon counter-rotates its orbit to preserve an upper-right opening of approximately 41.6 degrees, following only the orientation from the official flag construction diagonal.
